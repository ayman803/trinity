## [search] chk-noreduce: never reduce a move that gives check

**What.** In late-move reductions a checking move today gets one ply less
reduction (`r -= child.in_check()`) and is then reduced by the rest of
the table; the child extends by one (the check extension). Variant: a
move that gives check is never reduced (r = 0), so it is searched at full
depth plus the extension.

**Why.** no-checkext (-4.2 +/- 3.8 after 9000, [-4, 1], close to a fail)
says that following checks is worth more than the quarter of the nodes
the extension costs. Checking moves are also the moves most often
reduced into oblivion in reduced lines: at r = 3 or 4 deep in a
non-PV subtree the extension cannot undo the reduction. Giving checks the
same treatment the engine already gives the hash move (never reduced)
is the smallest change in the direction the evidence points.

**Measured today (cloud builds of today's main).** Bench 1156846 vs
982007 (+17.8% nodes to depth 10; checks are frequent in the bench
positions). On 60 held-out game positions at depth 10: nodes ratio median
1.002, mean 1.124 (min 0.56, max 2.78: check-rich positions explode,
quiet ones are untouched); best move differs in 14 of 60. At 300 ms the
depth reached is the same (median 14 vs 14; deeper 18, equal 26,
shallower 16). So the cost is near zero in the typical position and
large in a few.

**Test.** 8+0.08, [0, 5], GitHub. Bench changes, so the full test.

**Prediction.** Self-play -4 to +5; about 20% to pass (scaled 0.6x).
Kept against rated engines: about 50%; the check extension's value could
grow with depth (more perpetual-check defences found), so a pass gets a
40/60 night.

**Risk.** The explosive positions: a move that gives check in a position
with many checks (queen and rook against a bare king) now costs a full
search each; the time-loss watch is open, so the test's 0-time-loss
condition matters. If it fails, the opposite reading (checks are already
searched enough; the extension's value is in the child, not the parent)
closes this direction.

## Measured, no finding: table size and nodes to depth

Yesterday's question (every test runs with Hash 16 MB; a 40/15 move puts
20 times more nodes through the table). Main at depth 20-21 on 13 held-out
positions, 4-13 million nodes each, tables of 16, 64, 256 and 1024 MB:
the node count to a fixed depth moves by up to 50% either way between
table sizes with no consistent direction (6 positions at depth 21 favoured
16 MB five times; 7 positions at depth 20 gave 256/16 median 1.00, 1024/16
median 0.99). The same chaos the ablation audit saw with 8 vs 64 MB. A
no-IIR build showed the same spread (and 26% more nodes at 16 MB: IIR
earns its keep). Conclusion: at these node counts the table size does not
systematically change the search; nothing to fix, the "calibration at the
tester's hash setting" check stays as logged.

## Move-ranking metric, 1501 positions: not a usable gate

Yesterday's run (397 positions) put the two clear losers (control -9.7,
new-data -10.9) apart from the rest on one measure, the gap between the
network's own favourite move and the search's choice. At 1501 positions
(sampled across the held-out set, 6+ legal moves, no mate scores; each
child resolved by the network's depth-1 search; referee main's depth-7
search of the same children):

| network | Elo vs main | top-1 | top-3 | tau | gap (cp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| main | 0 | 29.3% | 45.4% | 0.595 | 95.2 |
| long900 | +7.0 | 30.3% | 45.6% | 0.594 | 95.0 |
| Seed2 (kb16) | +1.2 | 28.6% | 44.3% | 0.582 | 101.5 |
| avg 550-600 | +0.4 | 28.2% | 44.5% | 0.587 | 102.0 |
| Control2 | -0.2 | 30.3% | 45.9% | 0.593 | 91.5 |
| avg 450-600 | -1.3 | 28.0% | 44.5% | 0.586 | 101.1 |
| kb16 | -2.1 | 28.6% | 44.3% | 0.587 | 102.7 |
| control | -9.7 | 29.2% | 44.5% | 0.589 | 106.0 |
| new-data | -10.9 | 28.9% | 44.5% | 0.586 | 102.7 |

The two losers still sit at the bottom on the gap (106 and 103), but
Seed2 (+1.2), the averaged networks and kb16 now sit with them (101-103)
while Control2 (-0.2) is best (91.5); top-1 differs by less than its
standard error (about 1.2 points) across all eight; tau and top-3 the
same. Verdict: at this sample size the metric does not order networks
that are within 10 Elo of each other, and larger samples buy little
(the gap's spread is heavy-tailed). Like the result loss (yesterday's
section C), it would catch only a clearly broken network. Idea of 3
November closed: no tool to build. Lesson for the network lane: networks
within about 10 self-play Elo are indistinguishable by any cheap offline
measure tried (result loss in five slices, paired disagreements, move
ranking against the search); the 400-game screen plus the head-to-head
is the gate, and the GPU night is the real cost of a network experiment.
