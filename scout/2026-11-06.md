## [search] no-checkext: the check extension was never tested alone

**What.** `negamax` extends every node whose side to move is in check by
one ply, at every depth, with no limit along a line and no condition
(src/search.rs, "Check extension: never stop searching while in check").
It came with the first commit, and the removal campaign (Test queue, 21
October: IIR, futility, QS futility, negative extension, then the rest)
listed every pruning rule but not this extension; the ablation audit's 14
rules did not include it either. It is the last rule of the first commit
not tested on its own.

**Measured today (cloud, both builds from today's main, one thread).**
- Bench: main 982007 nodes (matches the logged bench); without the
  extension 633234: 36% fewer nodes to depth 10. Nodes per second equal
  (within noise).
- 60 positions from the held-out calibration games (every 2500th line of
  tools/net/data/heldout-positions.txt), `go depth 10`: nodes without /
  with the extension: median 0.74, mean 0.81 (min 0.09, max 1.80); time to
  depth 10 the same ratio (0.73 median).
- The same 60 at `go movetime 300`: depth reached median 15 vs 14 (mean
  16.4 vs 15.4); deeper in 37 positions, equal in 14, shallower in 9.
- Best moves agree in 45 of 60 at 300 ms (39 of 60 at depth 10): the
  search is genuinely different, not a relabelling of depth.

**Why it might gain.** The extension costs about a quarter of the nodes
per depth, i.e. about one ply of depth at a fixed time. By the time-odds
ruler (12% more time is about +6.8 self-play), a quarter more effective
depth is worth about +12 if the extended nodes were worth nothing. The
extension pays for itself only if the checks it follows at depth 1 and
above (where otherwise the in-check node would go to quiescence, which
already searches every evasion) find more than a ply of depth does.
Double counting: a checking move already gets one ply less reduction
(`r -= child.in_check()`), and then the child extends again, so a check
deep in a reduced line costs two plies of effort. Also, nothing limits
the extension along a line: a sequence of spite checks by a losing side
is searched to full depth at every check.

**Why it might lose.** Mating attacks and perpetual-check defences are
exactly what the extension sees; at 8+0.08 those decide many games.

**Cheap test.** Remove the four lines; bench changes (633234), so the full
test: 8+0.08, [-4, 1] as for the campaign (kept removed if not worse),
then the campaign's guard: a 40/60 night if it shows a clear gain (an
extension's value can change with depth). If it fails narrowly, the
follow-up variant is to keep the extension only where the checked side
has at most two evasions (the extension is then cheap and informative),
not a return to the unconditional rule.

**Prediction.** Self-play -12 to +12 (wide: the campaign gave +58, +60,
-7, -3 and -3 for five removals; the measured node saving sets the upper
half). About 35% to pass [-4, 1]. Kept against rated engines: about 50%
if it passes (speed-type gains have carried over about half).

**Risk.** A -7 like no-fut is as likely as a gain; the test is cheap and
the removal campaign's accounting (the pre-campaign 40/10 check) already
covers "small losses adding up".

## [network] lowlr-tail: was long900's gain more epochs, or more time at a low learning rate?

**What.** long900 (+5.65 vs Control2, same seed and data) changed one
thing, the length, but the cosine schedule was stretched with it
(trainer: `CosineDecayLR { final_superbatch: superbatches }`), so the run
had both 1.5x more passes over the data and 1.5x more steps near the
floor (1% of the start rate). Those are different levers with different
costs: more passes means long1350 (11 hours, needs the resumable
training of 4 November); more low-rate steps costs about an hour.

**Cheap, decisive test.** Continue Control2's saved 600 checkpoint for
100 superbatches at a constant 0.00001 (the floor the cosine reached), on
the same shuffled files, with the trainer's existing `finetune` path and
start lr 0.00001 (its cosine then runs from the floor downwards, i.e. a
flat tail). No restart at a high rate: NETWORK.md's "king buckets,
longer" lesson (kb-130 to kb-160 lost -13.8 and -3.8 after restarting at
a high learning rate) is the confound this design avoids. About 50
minutes of GPU (30 s per superbatch), a daytime idle slot under rule 17.
Then the usual screen and head-to-head against Control2, as for long900.
- If it gains (held-out loss aside: it could not see long900's +5.65
  either), the cheap lever is the tail: give every future run a longer
  low-rate tail instead of more hours, and long1350 is not the next step.
- If it does nothing, the gain was the extra passes, and long1350 with
  resumable training is the right next step.
Either answer is worth the hour. Needs: Control2's checkpoint still on
the PC (checkpoints\...control2...-600; if it was deleted, skip this and
go straight to long1350) and a small tools/net script (approval).

**Why it might work.** In stochastic training the weights wander with a
step proportional to the learning rate; the final error has a part that
shrinks only as the rate does. If Control2 stopped before that part had
settled, 100 more steps at the floor remove it at no cost in passes over
the data. Outside-chess source: standard results on learning-rate
annealing in stochastic gradient methods.

**Prediction.** Self-play vs Control2 -2 to +5; about 25% to pass [0, 5].
Kept against rated engines: unknown for networks of this kind (long900's
calibration night showed nothing yet); say 50%.

**Risk.** Adam's running averages are not reloaded by `finetune` and
re-warm over the first few hundred batches: harmless at this rate.
Memorisation: 100 more superbatches on the same data is 1.1 passes more;
the 2048 lesson (training positions 2.5% better, held-out 1.3%) says the
network memorises this file; a tail that only memorises would show as a
training-loss gain without a game gain, which the screen catches.
