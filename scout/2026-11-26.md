## Idea 1: [search] tm-hump: spend the clock where thinking pays

### The measurement

For a position from Trinity's own games: main's best move with N nodes
and with 2N nodes. If they differ, both moves are judged by a search four
times deeper than the second (on the position after each move). The gain
is counted in expected game points (the judge's two scores turned into
points with the scale that fits Trinity's results, 23 November), zero
when the move did not change, and averaged over all positions of that
move number. It answers one question: how much does a doubling of
thinking time buy at this stage of the game?

| move number | doubling from 200,000 nodes (450 positions each) | doubling from 800,000 nodes (380 each) |
|---|---|---|
| 9-14 | +2.1 +/- 0.8 points per 1,000 moves | 0.0 +/- 0.4 (move changed 15%: better 21, worse 23, equal 12) |
| 15-20 | +3.7 +/- 1.2 | +3.7 +/- 1.0 for moves 15-30 (changed 16%: better 37, worse 16) |
| 21-30 | +3.9 +/- 0.9 | (same row) |
| 31-40 | +2.7 +/- 1.0 | +1.5 +/- 0.7 |
| 41-60 | +1.2 +/- 0.6 | not run |
| 61 on | +1.7 +/- 0.9 | not run |

Two separate samples at two depths agree: at moves 15 to 30 a doubling
is worth about 3.7; in the first six moves after the book it is worth
between nothing and half of that, and the deeper run says nothing (the
search changes its mind as often as elsewhere, but the new move is no
better). After move 40 it is worth about a third.

### Where the time goes today

Seconds per move in main's own games (lost-game files of 20 and 23
November; time per move depends little on the result):

| clock | moves 1-10 | 11-20 | 21-30 | 31-40 | 41-50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10+0.1 | 0.43 | 0.35 | 0.26 | 0.20 | 0.16 |
| 40/60 (CCRL's kind) | 1.31 | 1.25 | 1.13 | 1.25 | 1.11 |

The reason is in the rule itself (src/search.rs, setup_time): with an
increment the plan is clock/25 plus most of the increment, so it is
largest when the clock is fullest, right after the book; with moves to
go it is clock/(moves to go + 1), the same for every move of the period.
So Trinity thinks longest, or equally long, exactly where thinking helps
least, and gives moves 15 to 30 no more than the rest.

It matches the losses: the referee's verdicts on the tournament games
(ESTIMATES.md, 25 November) put the ground lost at 15...a5, 17...c5 and
20...Ra6 in #166 and at 19...Nf8, 22...Ne6 and 28...Nxe3 in #117: moves
15 to 28.

One observation, not a reason: in the same game files Hobbes, Prune and
Patricia think hardest right after the book (2.0 s at moves 6-10 falling
to 0.6 s at 36-40 at 40/60). Trinity's own positions point the other
way, so this shape is not taken from anyone.

### The change

One multiplier on the planned think, by move number, in both kinds of
clock:

| move number | multiplier |
|---|---|
| up to 14 | 0.6 |
| 15-30 | 1.25 |
| 31-40 | 0.85 |
| 41 on | 1.0 (unchanged) |

Over a 40-move period with the book ending at move 8 this spends the
same total (6 x 0.6 + 16 x 1.25 + 10 x 0.85 = 32.1 moves' worth of 32).
It is about halfway between today's plan and the split the table alone
would give (time in proportion to the gain), because the first row is
the least certain. The hard limit can stay where it is for the early
moves, so a move that keeps changing can still take its time.

### Why it might work

The equimarginal principle (economics, operations research): a fixed
budget is best spent so that the last unit buys the same everywhere. It
is the idea of 14 October (scout/2026-10-14-2.md, "equal-marginal-value
test"), which proposed to find the shape with three game tests and was
never run. Today's measurement finds the shape directly and cheaply, and
it is not the shape guessed then.

### Cheap test

Time only, bench unchanged. As for tm-floor2: SPRT [0, 5] at 8+0.08 and
at 40/10 (the repeating clock is CCRL's kind), 0 time losses, and the
time-use tool on the test games to confirm the three bands came out as
planned.

### Prediction (before any game)

Self-play +1 to +5 at 8+0.08 and 0 to +4 at 40/10; about 20% to pass
[0, 5] (already scaled by 0.6). Arithmetic: if a doubling of all time is
worth about 40 and the moves share it in proportion to the table, moving
time as above is worth about +3 at either clock. Kept against rated
engines: most of it (tm-control's +6.8 in self-play showed as +16 in
calibration).

### Risks, and what could be wrong with the numbers

- The judge is a search too. If the value of an early move only shows
  thirty moves later, neither the judge nor a doubled search sees it, so
  the first row may be too low. But then extra time there would not find
  it either.
- Depth: CCRL's games are searched about 30 times deeper than my deeper
  run. Between the two runs the pattern sharpened; beyond that it is not
  measured.
- "Up to move 14" is tied to a book that ends near move 8. A much longer
  book would put the engine's first own moves straight into the 1.25
  band, which is the safe direction.
- Less time early means less in sharp book lines; the 40/60 losses to
  Hobbes mostly start from a bad book position (25 November).
- My record on time rules: tm-nodefloor failed (-2.8). The two time
  changes that passed (tm-control, tm-floor2) spent more time overall;
  this one only moves it.

## Idea 2: [search] tb-on-off: are the tablebases a gain or a cost?

### What is known

- CCRL plays every game with 3-4-5-piece tablebases, and so do the
  calibrations (scripts/calibrate.ps1 passes the folder to all engines).
  Every merge test runs without them: scripts/sprt.ps1 and the GitHub
  workflow pass no SyzygyPath.
- The gain has never been measured on its own. The row of 5 October in
  ESTIMATES.md ends "TB-off calibration pending to separate the two";
  it never ran. The release checklist (item 4) asks for exactly this
  number.

### Three weak signs that they may cost

- 5 October: tables were switched on together with a network that had
  gained +6 in self-play, and the calibration fell from 3390 to 3377.
  Against akimbo, which has no tablebase support and so did not change:
  -6 +/- 10.
- Tournament game #152: about 180K nodes per second in a 6-piece ending,
  against 1.1 to 1.2M in the same event (advisor's note).
- Trinity's own calibration games (tables on), lost-game files of 20 and
  23 November: its depth in the game against the depth today's main
  reaches here, without tables, on the same position and about the same
  number of nodes (60 positions per row at 10+0.1, 19 to 60 at 40/60):

  | pieces on the board | 10+0.1: game / here | 40/60: game / here |
  |---|---|---|
  | 19-26 | 15 / 13 | 18 / 17 |
  | 11-13 | 14 / 13 | 19 / 18 |
  | 9-10 | 14 / 13 | 19 / 19 |
  | 8 | 12 / 13 | 15 / 15 |
  | 7 | 12 / 12 (median difference +1) | 14 / 14 |
  | 6 | 10 / 11 | too few |

  In the middlegame the PC is about a ply ahead of the cloud; with 6 to
  8 pieces that lead is gone or reversed. Read as one to two plies lost
  where the tables come into reach. Medians of small whole numbers, so
  weak. (The fall from 19 to 14 by itself is the positions, not the
  tables: the cloud shows the same fall without them.)

None of the three is proof. Together they are enough to stop assuming
the tables help.

### What the engine does

In the search (not at the root, any node with depth left, not in the
quiescence search), right after a capture or pawn move that leaves 5 or
fewer pieces, it takes a lock, copies the board into the library's own
position type and looks the result up (src/tb.rs, probe_wdl). The answer
is exact; the cost is some microseconds, several ordinary nodes' worth,
and in endings where most captures lead to 5 pieces it is paid at a
large share of the nodes.

### Cheap test

On the PC (the cloud cannot fetch the tables: the network policy refuses
the hosts): main with tablebases against main without, 6,000 to 9,000
self-play games at 10+0.1, and 1,000 to 2,000 at 40/60. Report the
number with its error bar; it is a measurement, not a merge test. One
way that needs no script change: a test branch, never merged, whose only
difference is that the table folder is on by default, run with the
approved sprt.ps1 as -Dev against main. The Session knows the runner's
paths and may know a simpler way.

### Prediction (before any game)

Tables on against off at 10+0.1: -6 to +4, middle -1; at 40/60: -3 to
+5. About 35% that they cost 2 or more at the fast clock.

### What follows

- +2 or more: nothing to fix. The number goes into the 1.2 release
  checks; item 4 is then measured.
- About 0 or negative: the knowledge is being paid for with speed. Then
  (a) the fixed costs of one lookup in Trinity's own code (the lock, the
  board copy), which is ordinary engineering; (b) doing the lookup only
  where it is cheaper than searching, that is with enough depth left.
  As far as I know (b) is common practice in engines that use these
  tables. I have not looked at any, but it carries the originality flag
  and is Ayman's to allow or not.
- The gain is whatever the measurement shows is lost, and it is on
  CCRL's own conditions, so nothing is discounted for "kept against
  rated engines".

### Risks

Tables decide few games, so the error bar stays near +/- 4 even after
9,000. Fast self-play overstates speed and understates knowledge
compared with CCRL's clock; that is why the 40/60 run is in the plan.
The October sign is from a much older engine.
