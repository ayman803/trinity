## [search] Idea: matchup forensics, find what Prune exploits

**What it is.** Run the existing loss forensics (16-Loss-Forensics /
the runner's forensics job, Stockfish as referee only) on Trinity's
losses split by opponent, Prune against the other three together, and
compare the profiles: phase of the game where the decisive mistake falls
(opening/middlegame/endgame by piece count), mistake type (search vs
evaluation, by the 10x-time retry), material balance when it happens, and
whether the position was closed or open (pawn count on the board is a
cheap proxy). A weakness that one opponent exploits consistently is a
specific, testable target; the same weakness then likely costs points
against the many engines on the CCRL list that play in a similar way.

**Why it might work.** Differential diagnosis (medicine) and
contrastive analysis: comparing a case group with a control group shows
what is specific to the cases, which pooled data hides. The loss
forensics so far pooled all opponents (68-75% "gradual" losses), which
is why it pointed nowhere precise. The Prune effect is about 2-3 times
the standard error of one run and repeats across eight runs, so it is a
real signal, not noise.

**Cheap test.** No new code if the forensics job can filter by opponent
(otherwise one option). The games are already saved: every calibration
publishes Trinity's lost games. About 300 Prune losses and 300 others from
the last week, a few hours of PC time on one night. Decision rule fixed
before: if one category (phase or type) is at least 1.5x more frequent in
Prune losses than in the others with at least 50 cases, it becomes the
target of the next search or evaluation candidate; otherwise drop it.

**Prediction.** 0 Elo directly; about 50% that a clear category shows
up. If it does, the candidate it points to: +2 to +8 self-play, kept
against rated engines better than usual (60-80%), since it would be
chosen from games against rated engines rather than self-play.

**Risk.** A night of PC time. The difference may be Prune's strength
(it is the highest-rated of the four) rather than a style matchup: check
first whether Prune's implied rating gap stays after allowing for its
rating (it does above: the table compares implied Trinity ratings, which
already account for each opponent's own rating).
