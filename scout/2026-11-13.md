- The scale is not uniform: wdl25 / Control2 is 0.91 with 2-10 pieces,
  0.84 with 11-16, 0.81 with 17-24 and 0.79 with 25-32. A network
  trained three-quarters on the search score and one quarter on the
  result is flatter where the result is far away (openings and
  middlegames) and nearly the same in endgames. That is what the change
  was meant to do; it also means the pruning margins, tuned to today's
  scale, are effectively 15-25% wider for this network in the phases
  where most pruning happens.
- Result loss 1.7% worse as it stands; with its eval multiplied by a
  constant k it reaches Control2's loss at k of about 1.25 (k 1.18:
  0.08156; 1.25: 0.08131; 1.35: 0.08108). So this is not a broken run:
  it predicts results as well as Control2 once rescaled, and by design
  it predicts them less sharply at its own scale.

**Suggested for the test (For the Session, below).** Two 400-game
screens, not one: wdl25 as it is, and wdl25 with its output scaled by
1.2 on the test branch (one constant where `nnue::evaluate` returns, the
same place the 2048 check looked). Take the better into the head-to-head
against Control2. A constant cannot undo the phase dependence (after
x1.2 the endgame scale is 1.09 and the opening 0.94), so if the network
wins at all it will need the usual SPSA re-tune before its merge, which
rule 5 requires for networks anyway. Without the scaled screen a loss
would not tell whether the recipe or the scale lost.

## Not proposed: the 50-move drift of the eval

`evaluate` scales every eval by (200 - halfmove)/200 (0.9 at 20 plies
without a capture or pawn move, 0.8 at 40, 0.5 at 99; src/search.rs
"Drift towards a draw as the fifty-move counter grows"). It is older than
the repository's visible history and has no test in ESTIMATES.md, so it
is the last first-commit rule never tested alone. On the held-out
positions it touches 10% (halfmove 20 or more, scale 0.9 or less) and 6%
(40 or more, 0.8 or less), nearly all endgames. Its purpose, making the
search prefer moves that reset the counter in won endgames, is sound,
and removing it would mostly add 50-move draws in won positions. Not
worth a test; recorded so nobody re-finds it.
