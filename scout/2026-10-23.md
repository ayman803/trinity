## [search] Idea: measure whether the evaluation is too pessimistic when behind, and set all "behind" margins from that measurement

**What it is.** A pruning margin is a bet that the static evaluation will
not be wrong by more than the margin. The results above say that bet was
lost far more often on the losing side than on the winning side. Measure
that directly: on a few thousand positions from Trinity's own games, take
the static evaluation and a deeper search score, and compare the error
(search minus static) separately for positions where the side to move is
behind (static below alpha by 100, 200, 400 cp) and ahead by the same
amounts. If the errors are lopsided (when behind, the search usually finds
much more than the static eval thought; when ahead, it rarely finds much
less), then:
1. set every remaining "behind" margin (main-search futility, which just
   gained +5.8 when widened, quiet-move pruning by move count or
   exchange value when below alpha) from the measured error, for example
   the 95th percentile of the error at that depth, instead of by hand;
2. leave the "ahead" margins alone, or tighten them, as rfp-wide's -10
   suggests.

**Why it might work.** Forecast verification in weather and medicine:
a forecast can be well calibrated on average and still be biased in one
tail (reliability diagrams split by forecast value show this). A static
evaluation behind in material has tactical resources (sacrifices,
checks, counterattacks) that a quiet evaluation can't see; when it is
ahead, the opponent's resources are what the search must find, and the
null-move/RFP machinery already handles that side. Two removals worth
about +60 each on the "behind" side are strong evidence that the tail is
lopsided.

**Cheap test.** Measurement first (offline, minutes, no games): the
error tables by side and depth. Pass condition for the idea to continue:
the 95th-percentile error when behind is clearly larger (say 1.5x) than
when ahead. Then one candidate: futility (and any other remaining
"behind" pruning) margins set from the table. SPRT [0, 5] at 8+0.08, plus
40/10 because pruning gains have shrunk with more time before.

**Prediction.** If the table is lopsided: +3 to +10 self-play on top of
fut-wide; kept against rated engines about 60% (the 40/10 test of the
campaign kept nearly all of it: +51 at 40/10). If not lopsided: drop it;
the two big removals were then about those rules' own faults, not about
the evaluation.

**Risk.** Release 1.1 freezes today: any candidate from this belongs to
the next version, not 1.1. The measurement must use positions from real
games (not bench), at the depths where the rules act (1-8).
