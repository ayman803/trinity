Together about -4.0 +/- 2.9. Moving a third of the thinking time from the
first moves after the book to moves 15 to 30 costs strength at both
clocks. The forecast was wrong in sign, not only in size.

What that says about the measurement behind it. I measured what a
doubled search finds at each stage of the game, judged by a search four
times deeper, each position on a fresh hash table. Even its own column
for the test's depth (doubling from 200,000 nodes: 2.1 at moves 9-14,
3.7 to 3.9 at 15-30) forecast a small plus. The games say about -4, so
the measure is off by about 6. It does not tell where clock time is
worth most. Likely reasons, none of them tested:
- the judge is a search too, and cannot see the long-term worth of an
  early move (the risk I listed first yesterday);
- a fresh hash per position ignores that early thinking is inherited by
  the moves that follow;
- the first moves out of an unbalanced book matter more than their
  search scores show (most losses to Hobbes start there, 25 November).

The reverse change, thinking more on the early moves, is not a candidate
either. Near the best split a change is worth little in either direction
(under 2 by the same arithmetic that was wrong by 6), and it is what the
opponents do, so it would not pass the originality rule on Trinity's own
evidence.

## tb-on-off: no cost at the fast clock; the slow clock is not settled

| clock | my forecast | result |
|---|---|---|
| 10+0.1 | -6 to +4 | -0.09 +/- 3.81 after 8000 games |
| 40/60 | -3 to +5 | -11.2 +/- 11.1 after 960 games |

At the fast clock the tablebases neither cost nor gain: the three weak
signs I listed yesterday did not add up to a loss. The 40/60 number is
outside my range, but on 960 games it is two error bars from zero, and
my own guess is that most of it is luck: a lookup that was too slow
would show at both clocks.

One thing in the code changes how this test should be read. With 5 or
fewer pieces on the board, Trinity with tablebases does not search at
all: it plays the table's move at once (src/search.rs, search_parallel;
src/tb.rs, root_move). When the table says "draw", the move it gets is,
by the library's own rule, the first quiet drawing move: no pawn push
and no capture while a quiet drawing move exists. So as the stronger
side in a drawn ending (rook and pawn against rook, say) it shuffles to
the 50-move draw and never tries to win. Trinity without tablebases
keeps pressing and sometimes wins such endings against an opponent who
can go wrong.
- In this test the opponent has no tablebases, so the tables' side
  gives up those chances and the other side keeps them.
- At CCRL nearly every opponent has tablebases and holds a table draw
  whatever Trinity plays, so nothing is given up there.
So this test can only under-read what the tables are worth at CCRL. A
small minus here need not be a minus there. This is reasoning from the
code, not a measurement.

How to settle the slow clock, fixed now, before more games:
- continue 40/60 to 4,000 games in all (about +/- 5.4): roughly 13 more
  chunks of 240, two idle nights after tonight's calibration, and stop
  there whatever it shows;
- "tables cost at slow clocks" only if the whole range is then below
  zero. Anything else: logged as no measurable cost, release-check item
  4 measured, topic closed;
- if it is below zero: the comparison that matters for CCRL is then a
  cheaper lookup against today's, with tablebases on for BOTH sides
  (this test cannot give that), starting with the fixed costs of one
  lookup in Trinity's own code.
