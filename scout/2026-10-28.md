## [search] Idea: follow the mechanism #38 found, prune late quiet moves by their place in the order

**What it is.** Three results now point the same way:
- dropping all quiets after the first pruned one: **+18.6**;
- pruning quiets later by move count (lmp-wide): **-5.4**;
- pruning by history score alone (hist-prune, twice): about 0.

So pruning late quiet moves by their *position in the move order*,
once the order says "the rest are worse than one already judged
prunable", is worth a lot; pruning by a separate score is not. Two cheap
candidates follow:
1. **lmp-tight**: start move-count pruning earlier (the opposite of
   lmp-wide), one step at a time, for example the move-count limit
   lowered by about 20%.
2. **drop-keep-checks**: the drop rule as it is, but a quiet move that
   gives check is still searched. If the drop's gain comes from cutting
   genuinely bad late quiets, this costs little and protects tactics; if
   it costs a lot, the drop is gaining partly by not searching checks,
   which is worth knowing before tuning anything else around it.

**Why it might work.** In ranking and search problems outside chess
(information retrieval, sequential testing), a good ordering makes the
rank itself the most reliable signal: once the k-th item fails a test,
items ranked below it rarely pass. The move ordering is now good enough
(#38, speed-pick2) for that to hold, and the +18.6 says it does.

**Cheap test.** Two SPRTs at 8+0.08 [0, 5] on GitHub (both a few lines).
If either passes, 40/10 as well, since pruning gains have shrunk at
slower clocks before (main's gap: +111 at 10+0.1, +63 at 40/60).

**Prediction.** lmp-tight: -2 to +6, about 35% to pass.
drop-keep-checks: -4 to +3, about 20% to pass (its main value is the
answer). Kept against rated engines about 50-60%.

**Risk.** lmp-wide's -5.4 says the current limit is not too tight, but
not that tighter is better: the optimum may be where it is. Keep the
step small.
