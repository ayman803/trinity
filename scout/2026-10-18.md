## [search] Idea: interleave the candidate and the reference within one night

**What it is.** Today a night runs 1.0.0 for 2.4 hours, then main for 2.4
hours. Anything that changes between 1 am and 5 am (Windows maintenance
tasks, temperature, a background update) lands entirely on one of the two
and shows up as Elo. Instead, alternate them in small blocks, for example
5 rounds of 1.0.0, 5 of main, 5 of 1.0.0, ..., with the same opening
sequence for both, and compute the gap block by block.

**Why it might work.** Basic experimental design from agriculture and
medicine (blocking and randomisation): compare treatments inside the same
block of conditions rather than in separate periods, and pair them on the
same material (here the same openings). Two facts from this week say the
conditions matter here: 1.0.0 measured 3466 by day and 3478 at night with
nothing changed, and all four opponents moved the same way. Interleaving
turns slow drift into noise that cancels in the difference; pairing by
opening removes the part of the variance that comes from which openings
happened to be drawn.

**Cheap test.** No new engine work: a calibration option that takes two
engines and alternates rounds (the opponents, book, Syzygy and time control
stay the same). Check on one night: run 1.0.0 against itself interleaved
(an A-vs-A test). The gap should come out within about +/- 5 of zero, and
the block-to-block spread shows the real noise. Then use it for every
candidate night.

**Prediction.** 0 Elo directly. The gap's error should fall from about
+/- 8.5 to about +/- 6 for the same 8000 games, and drift stops being a
hidden error. That matters most for the stop rule (+10 on calibration in
4 weeks), which is now about the same size as one gap's error.

**Risk.** A script change needs one approval. If the A-vs-A night shows a
gap far from zero, the problem is in the method, not in the engine: find
it before trusting any interleaved gap.
