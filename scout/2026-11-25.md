## 1. "0.00, then a collapse": measured and dropped

In #117 Trinity 1.0.0 gave up a rook for an attack it scored 0.00 for
five moves (21...hxg4 to 25...Nf3+) and lost. A draw score is a special
number: it says "I can force a repetition". Is that Trinity's least
reliable number?

**In lost games it looks that way.** Trinity's own moves with a score
within 1.0, by kind of score; "collapse" = its eval is at least 1.0
lower within its next three moves.

| lost games | draw score (0.00, +-0.01) | other score up to 0.30 | 0.31 to 1.00 | lost games with a draw score then a collapse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| main, 10+0.1, 20 Nov (305) | 25% of 685 moves | 12% | 12% | 96 (31%) |
| 1.0.0, 10+0.1, 20 Nov (1028) | 26% of 2890 | 14% | 15% | 431 (42%) |
| main, 40/60, 17 and 23 Nov (68) | 25% of 125 | 14% | 14% | 18 (26%) |
| 1.0.0, 40/60, 17 and 23 Nov (205) | 21% of 579 | 12% | 14% | 66 (32%) |

A collapse follows a draw score about twice as often as any other small
score, in both versions and at both clocks.

**Over all games it does not hold.** 26,000 held-out positions with 8 or
more pieces (games won, drawn and lost alike), today's main, 60,000
nodes each. The search gave a draw score in 1,887 (7.3%). How those
games ended for the side to move, by what the raw network eval said:

| search says draw, raw eval is | positions | won | drawn | lost | score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| -2.0 or worse | 167 | 4% | 81% | 14% | 0.449 +/- 0.016 |
| -2.0 to -1.0 | 175 | 10% | 84% | 6% | 0.523 |
| -1.0 to -0.3 | 357 | 6% | 85% | 8% | 0.492 |
| within 0.3 | 551 | 10% | 80% | 10% | 0.500 |
| +0.3 to +1.0 | 385 | 7% | 85% | 8% | 0.497 |
| +1.0 to +2.0 | 176 | 6% | 86% | 7% | 0.494 |
| +2.0 or better | 76 | 16% | 79% | 5% | 0.553 +/- 0.026 |

For comparison, an ordinary search score of -0.30 to -0.02 scored 0.481
(68% drawn) and +0.02 to +0.30 scored 0.520 (70% drawn). So a draw score
is, if anything, the more dependable number: the game was drawn 80 to
86% of the time, and the result sits at 0.49 to 0.52 whatever the raw
eval says, except at the two ends (raw eval 2 pawns or more away from
the draw), where it leans 0.05 towards the raw eval. Those two ends are
0.9% of all positions.

Three more checks, all on main's 305 lost games of 20 November:

- Not caused by positions from earlier in the game. Of 259 draw scores
  reproduced here with the game's moves fed in, 229 (88%) are still a
  draw score with the history removed.
- Thinking longer does not help more there. With 16 times the nodes
  (200,000 to 3.2 million) the best move changed in 31% of the positions
  with a draw score and in 32% of those with an ordinary small score.
- Trinity does not rush these moves: time used was 0.97 of its
  neighbouring moves, the same as for any other move.

Reading: selection. A draw claim that fails, fails fast, so failed
claims pile up just before the collapse in lost games; the many claims
that hold are in the drawn games, which the lost-game files do not
contain. No candidate, and no time rule either. What is left (the two
ends of the table) is too small to test.

For #117 itself: an older scratch build (the 1.0.0 recipe network with
the search of early November) repeats the game's 0.00 at 25...Nf3+ up to
depth 25; today's main does not (see section 4).

## 2. Units: each opponent prints on its own scale

The advisor's note reads #166 as "Trinity's evals 4 to 7 times Hobbes's".
Checked on the games Trinity LOST in four calibration files (20 November
at 10+0.1 and 23 November at 40/60, main and 1.0.0), so the opponent's
edge was real in every one. For each Trinity move, its own number
against the number the opponent printed on its next move (medians):

| opponent (lost games) | Trinity says 0.3 to 1.0 against itself | 1.0 to 3.0 | 3.0 to 8.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bread (316) | opponent 1.14 (Trinity is 0.6 times) | 2.56 (0.7) | 4.93 (1.0) |
| akimbo (305) | 1.08 (0.6) | 2.24 (0.9) | 4.25 (1.1) |
| Prune (470) | 0.39 (1.6) | 0.90 (2.0) | 2.30 (2.2) |
| Patricia (325) | 0.38 (1.6) | 0.76 (2.4) | 1.45 (3.5) |
| Hobbes (82) | 0.17 (3.7) | 0.38 (4.6) | 1.16 (4.6) |

Reading:
- There is no common scale. Bread and akimbo print larger numbers than
  Trinity; Prune and Patricia about half; Hobbes about a quarter.
- Hobbes prints a quarter of Trinity's number in games Hobbes WINS. So
  "4 to 7 times" in #166 is Hobbes's scale. Trinity's -4 in that
  middlegame was right: by Trinity's own games (23 November file) a
  search score of 3.5 or more is followed by a loss 95 to 97% of the
  time, and this game was lost.
- It also re-reads #46: Hobbes's +0.30 there is about 1.2 in Trinity's
  units, which is what the referee said (-1.1). The real gap in that
  queen ending was -3.06 against about -1.2, as the Session already
  logged, not -3.06 against -0.30.

The 30 games main lost to Hobbes at 40/60 on 23 November (2 wins, 88
draws). For each: Trinity's eval at its first own move after the book,
and the move where its eval first settles at -1.0 or worse.

| lost to | games | first own move already -0.5 or worse | median first eval | eval settles at -1.0 by move (median) | sudden (level three moves before) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hobbes | 30 | 60% | -0.69 | 14 | 20% |
| Prune | 10 | 30% | -0.20 | 31 | 40% |

(1.0.0 against Hobbes, 52 losses: 42%, -0.39, move 21, 25%.)

Reading: against a stronger engine most losses are the bad side of an
opening pair. The book hands Trinity a position it rates -0.5 or worse
and Hobbes converts it; only one loss in five is a sudden drop from a
level game. That is what a stronger opponent looks like, not a specific
weakness. The tournament losses have the same shape: #108 (-1.1 at the
first own move) and #173 (-0.63 at the first own move, below).

## Considered and not proposed

- A time premium when the root score is a draw score: ruled out by
  section 1 (more nodes change the move no more often there).
- A wider network judged at a long clock: looked up, stays closed. The
  October post-mortem prices the 2048 network's eval gain at about 0
  before its speed cost, so a slower clock cannot turn it positive.
