### 2. Why -3.06 stood still for 28 moves in #46

Two things, both checked on the position after move 50 (queen and pawn
against queen and two pawns, Trinity a pawn down):

- The 50-move damping that Trinity already has does not bite here. The
  same position with the counter set to 0, 10, 40, 70 and 90 gives -3.09,
  -3.05, -3.13, -3.31 and -3.18 on main (fresh hash each time). With the
  counter at 90 a draw by the rule is only ten half-moves away, so the
  score can stay at -3 only because the stronger side's line contains a
  pawn move, which sets the counter back to 0. The damping works only
  once the stronger side has no pawn move left; in the game the number
  came down in steps (-2.47, -1.88, -1.07, -0.87, -0.2, 0.00) as those
  pawn moves were used up.
- With the hash table kept from move to move, as in a game, the
  1.0.0-recipe build repeats one number across moves 45 to 85: -2.77,
  -2.77, -2.77, -2.76, -2.75, -2.75. The search keeps meeting positions
  it has already scored. That is the flat line in the game; it was not a
  stuck search. (Main moves a little more: -2.68 to -3.17.)

Also: the search makes the number larger than the raw network eval here
(raw -1.7 to -2.7, search -2.7 to -3.4), not smaller.

Reading:
- The advisor's pattern is real in Trinity's own games. A search score
  of +1.5 with 6 to 9 pieces was followed by a draw nine times in ten
  (score 0.55); the same +1.5 in the middlegame scored 0.68 to 0.70.
  The search does not remove it.
- It is not a wrong scale. From 3.5 pawns up the number means the same in
  every phase (0.95 to 0.97). The gap is in small and medium evals (0.5
  to 3.5 pawns) with 13 pieces or fewer. About one game position in ten
  sits in the worst zone (13 pieces or fewer, eval 0.5 to 2.0).
- The training weight cannot fix it: the three WDL networks, put on one
  overall size, score the same in the 1.0-to-2.0 band with 6 to 9 pieces
  (0.54, 0.55, 0.56).

### 4. Part of the gap to the opponents' numbers is only units

In #24, where both engines agreed who was better, Trinity's number was
1.2 to 2 times its opponent's all the way (-0.33 against -0.16, -0.55
against -0.31, -1.17 against -0.80, -1.45 against -0.94). Each engine
prints its score in its own units; Trinity prints the network's number
as it is. That part costs nothing in play.

### Limits (what could be wrong with this)

- These are positions both engines chose to enter after deep searches.
  Many drawn endings are on the board because the defender's search had
  already seen the draw. So the tables show what the number meant for
  positions that were reached; they say less about positions inside the
  search tree, where the eval does its work.
- Fast games. At CCRL's clock more of these endings are held (#46), so
  there the gap is probably larger.
- In neither tournament game did the high number cost anything: #46 was
  held, and #53 was a blocked position with no way through.
- With 5 pieces or fewer the tablebases answer instead of the eval at
  CCRL, so the zone that matters is 6 to 13 pieces.

### Why no candidate

- A plain eval scale is not a candidate. EVAL_SCALE is already tuned by
  SPSA and every margin lives on the same scale, so another scale only
  changes the printed number.
- Shrinking the eval as pieces come off (the advisor's "drawishness
  damping term"; also the "endgame-only scale" pencilled in under the 21
  November note) is a device I recognise from other engines. I have read
  nothing about how anyone does it; I only know it exists. Under the
  originality rule that means drop. Only Ayman can make an exception, and
  on today's evidence I would not ask him: no lost points are shown, and
  my honest forecast for such a change is about 0 (range -4 to +4, about
  15% to pass [0, 5]). My three search nudges all failed; the margins
  inside endings are tuned to today's numbers; and the evidence comes
  from chosen positions. A plain endgame scale would also be the wrong
  shape: big evals are right in every phase.
- A network version (more game-result weight as pieces come off) I do
  not recognise from anywhere, but the numbers above rule it out: the
  whole range from WDL 0 to 0.5 moves endgame evals by about a quarter,
  against a gap of two to four times, and leaves the score per band
  where it was. A gain of +1 to +2 could not be shown in one night.

What would change this: tablebase truth on positions that were NOT chosen
by a search (see point 4 below).
