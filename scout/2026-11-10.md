## [search] tm-nodefloor: the "best move hogs the nodes" rule no longer shortens the think

**What.** Between iterations the soft time is scaled by three signals:
best-move stability (floored at 1.0 since tm-floor2, +4.1), the score
drop, and the share of nodes spent on the best move: `node_scale = (1.5 -
fraction) x 1.35`, which is below 1.0 whenever the best move took more
than 76% of the nodes (down to 0.81 at 100%). Variant: that scale is
floored at 1.0 as well; it still lengthens the think when the effort is
spread, it never shortens it. The 13 October design notes list this
constant among those "never tuned" (part C, stop-rule calibration).

**Measured today (cloud, 60 held-out positions, probe build of main).**
- The rule fires on most moves: the best move takes a median 86% of the
  nodes in the last iteration; node_scale is below 1.0 on 70% of moves at
  a 10 s + 0.1 s clock (65% at a 60 s clock) and below 0.9 on about 55%.
- Flooring it raises the mean move time by 12.6% at 10+0.1 (median
  position +6.4%; mean 461 -> 519 ms, mean depth 16.6 -> 17.0) and by 19%
  at a 60 s clock (median +9%; depth 21.3 -> 21.9). So most of the change
  is "think longer on the moves the search is sure about".

**Why it might gain.** Two of Trinity's own results: tm-control (+6.8 for
12% more time on every move: the unspent-time leak is real; the 19%
unused at game end in the 13 October data) and tm-floor2 (+4.1 for
removing one of the two shortening signals). This removes the other.
First principles: a best move that absorbs 86% of the nodes is one the
search has stopped questioning, which is when a refutation one ply
deeper is cheapest to miss; the signal cannot tell "obviously best"
from "nothing else was looked at".

**Test.** 8+0.08 vs main, [0, 5], GitHub, with the usual 0-time-loss
condition (it spends more; tm-lowclock and the 1 s reserve are in place).
Optional control, as for tm-unsure: 12.6% more soft time on every move;
if it matches the floor, the targeting adds nothing and the gain is the
leak alone (still a gain, still merged by the usual rule). If the floor
passes, a 40/10 run as for tm-floor2: repeating controls carry over less
of such gains (tm-stable-floor +3.1 at 40/10).

**Prediction.** Self-play +2 to +7, about 35% to pass [0, 5] (tm-control
and tm-floor2 passed on the same leak; scaled for the record). Kept
against rated engines at 40/15: about 30% (time gains have shrunk most
at repeating controls).

**Risk.** More time per move drains a sudden-death clock sooner: the
time-loss watch (1-2 per 4000 at 10+0.1) must not worsen; the test
counts them. At CCRL's 40/15 the clock resets every 40 moves, so the
drain risk is a self-play artefact, but the gain may be too.
